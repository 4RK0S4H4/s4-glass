Wide-Screen Layout Samples

The same app at the width of a 1080p monitor (1920) and a 1440p monitor (2560), shown as a scaled-down but real page. Pick A, B or C at the top, then switch screen and view. Slide the picture sideways on a phone; turn the phone to landscape for a bigger view. Density follows the rules you set: Compact at 1080p, Standard at 1440p.

A · Fill the Screen (today). Nothing is capped: tables and cards stretch to the edge, and the detail panel stops at 600px. On 1440p the description and the amount end up about 1,500px apart, so the eye loses its row.

B · Cap and Centre. Everything stops at 1600px and sits in the middle, with empty margins either side. Rows are easy to follow, but on a list with a panel the list shrinks to about 1000px and 1440p wastes a third of the screen.

C · Cap, Then Widen the Panel. Overview and list pages stop at 1600px and centre. On a list with a panel the list keeps filling and the surplus goes to the detail panel, which grows to 34% of the width (up to 880px) and sets out its cards in two columns. Extra width is used for the working area instead of stretching rows.

RecommendedC · Cap, Then Widen the Panel. Reconciliation is done in the detail panel, so the extra width of a 1440p monitor is worth most there, while overview pages are read like a page and shouldn't stretch past about 1600px. At 1080p it behaves almost like today.
Recommendation basis: my own design judgement (comfortable line length, where the work happens on a big monitor). The front-end design skill was not available in this session, so it was not used.
