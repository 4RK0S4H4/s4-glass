Table Samples

Three different ways to work a table of 240 transactions (fictional data). In every option the header and the first columns stay put when you scroll sideways, and the amount stays on the right. Pick A, B or C at the top, then try it: filter, tick rows and watch the total.

A · Toolbar and Pages. The familiar one: a search box, status chips and an account drop-down above the table; 25 rows to a page; tick rows and the toolbar turns into a selection bar (count, total, actions). Works the same on a phone.

B · Spreadsheet Style. Closest to Excel: each column header has its own filter (the funnel); rows are denser; click a row to select it, Shift-click a range, Ctrl-click to add; endless scroll; and a status bar at the bottom always shows the count, sum and average of what is selected (or of everything shown). Fast for power users; harder to discover, and on a phone it needs a different filter sheet.

C · Day Ledger. Rows grouped by day, each day with its own subtotal, like the bank's statement, so you can tie each day out. Filters sit in a side rail with live counts for every value. Tick a day to select all its rows; the selection total docks under the table. "Show Earlier Days" loads more. On a phone the rail turns into a row above the table.

RecommendedC · Day Ledger. Reconciling is a tie-out job: you compare the app with a bank statement that is organised by day, so day groups with subtotals mirror the thing people check against and make a missing or doubled line obvious without adding anything up. The rail with counts answers "how many are left, and where?" before anyone clicks, and the total next to the action stops mis-matches. Option B is the faster tool for a trained user, but nothing in it tells a newcomer what to do; A is the safest and the least helpful.
Recommendation basis: my own design judgement for finance work (scan paths, tie-out, discoverability, phone fallback). The front-end design skill was not available in this session, so it was not used.
