Table Samples

A table of 240 transactions with fictional data. Try it: search, filter by status or account, tick rows, tick the header box, and scroll sideways (the checkbox and description stay on the left, the amount on the right). Pick A, B or C at the top and see how paging and the selection bar change.

A · Pages, Bar on Top. 25 rows a page with "Rows per page", Previous and Next, and "26–50 of 240". Ticking a row swaps the filter bar for a selection bar (count, actions, Clear). The header tick selects the page, and a link offers all 240 matches.

B · Endless Scroll. 30 rows load, more arrive as you reach the bottom (and a Load More button for keyboards). No page numbers, so you lose your place less, but you can't say "row 180" or jump to the end, and the page gets heavy on a phone. Selection bar as in A.

C · Pages, Totals Below. Pages as in A, but the filter bar stays put, applied filters show as removable chips under it, and the selection appears as a bar docked under the table with the sum of the selected amounts next to the actions. For reconciliation you see what you're about to match without leaving the table.

RecommendedC · Pages, Totals Below. Reconciling is "pick these rows, check they add up, then act", so the running total belongs next to the action, and the filter bar shouldn't change under your hand. Pages keep the row position stable and let people say "page 3, row 4"; endless scroll doesn't.
Recommendation basis: my own design judgement for finance work (the running total next to the action, stable row positions, a filter bar that doesn't change under your hand). The front-end design skill was not available in this session, so it was not used.
