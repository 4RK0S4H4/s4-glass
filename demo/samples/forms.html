Form Samples

The same "New Payment" form, behaving three different ways. Try breaking it: leave fields empty, type a bad invoice number such as 12, pick a due date before the invoice date, then press the button at the bottom. Fictional data only.

A · Check as You Go. One page. A field is checked when you leave it, and a problem shows right under the field with an icon and a soft red fill. As soon as you fix it, the message goes and a green tick appears. Empty fields aren't flagged until you press Save, so tabbing through doesn't shout. Save jumps to the first problem.

B · Check on Submit. One page, nothing is checked while you type. Press Save and a red summary at the top lists every problem as a link that jumps to the field; the fields show their messages too, and the summary shrinks as you fix things. Quiet while you work; you meet all the problems at once.

C · One Step at a Time. The form is split into three short steps and a review. Each step is checked when you press Next, with the same messages as A, and Back never loses what you typed. The last screen lists everything with an Edit link per line before you send it.

RecommendedA · Check as You Go. This is the form people use all day (a rule, a lookup, a payment). Telling someone about a mistake the moment they leave the field, next to the field, is the fastest way for them to fix it, and not flagging untouched empty fields keeps it calm. Add B's summary only to very long forms (ten or more fields), where a list of problems helps more than hunting for red. Keep C for the rare long flow (an import, first-time setup): for a normal form, three screens and a review are more clicks than the form is worth.
Recommendation basis: my own design judgement (when an error is most useful, calm for people who tab through, accessibility of messages, how long the form is). The front-end design skill was not available in this session, so it was not used.
